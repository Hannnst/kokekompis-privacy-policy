Overview

KokeKompis (“the app”, “we”) is a personal recipe cookbook for Android. It extracts recipes from web pages you choose and stores them as plain text on your device. This policy explains what the app does with data. In short: it stores your recipes and photos on your own device, it contains no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it never sends anything about you anywhere.

Who we are

KokeKompis is an independent app developed by Hanna N. Støen. There is no company behind it, no account you can create, and no server you can log in to. We cannot see your recipes, your photos, or anything else stored on your device. The only thing that can reach us is what you choose to send us yourself — see the issue tracker.

What the app does not collect

The app has no accounts, no registration and no login. It contains no analytics, no advertising and no tracking software. There is no crash-reporting service, no telemetry and no usage statistics of any kind: nothing is collected about you in the background, and the app has no mechanism for building a profile of you.

Your recipes, your photos, the pages you have saved and the pages you have read are never sent to us or to anyone else.

There is no exception to this. If you want to report a problem or suggest an improvement, you can open an issue on GitHub — that is a conversation with GitHub, and the app plays no part in it.

What the app stores

Recipes you create, the cover photos you add — including recipe images the app has downloaded from the web — and your in-app preferences (theme, language, unit system, categories) are stored locally on your device, in the app’s private storage. The app also keeps a local database that it uses as a cache, so that it can show your library quickly. It contains no information about you.

Copies of the web pages the app fetches for you, and of the photos it displays, are kept temporarily in a cache folder (up to about 10 MB for the fetched pages). Android clears that folder itself when it needs space, and it is never included in a device backup.

Backups. If your device’s operating system performs Android backup or device-to-device transfer, it copies the folder that holds your recipes and their photos. The cache database and your preferences are deliberately excluded from that backup. You can control this in your Android system settings.

How long it is kept. This data stays on your device until you delete it or uninstall the app. We have no copy of it and no way to retrieve it, so uninstalling the app is equivalent to deleting everything. If you use a third-party sync or backup tool of your own choosing — for example Syncthing, Nextcloud or Git — the handling of that data is governed by that provider’s terms and privacy policy, not by this one.

Exporting and sharing

You can export all your recipes, or a selection of them, as a ZIP file. The app asks your system’s file picker where to save it, and it can also share that ZIP file with another app. You can print any recipe, and share a single recipe or a shopping list with other apps.

Once you have exported, shared or printed a recipe, that copy is outside the app’s control. It is then handled by the app you exported it to, or by whoever you shared it with, under their terms — not under this policy.

Network access

The app reaches the network in four situations, and only in these four:

  1. When you give it a URL to extract a recipe. The app fetches that page directly from the site you chose.
  2. When it loads a recipe photo that is still hosted on the web. The app normally downloads the photo once, while extracting the recipe, and stores it with the recipe. If that download failed, or if you imported a recipe whose photo is hosted on the web, the photo is fetched again over the network whenever it is shown or printed.
  3. When you use translation or language detection for the first time. The ML Kit libraries then download the language model they need from Google. The download is a file transfer from Google; it contains none of your recipes, and the translation itself is performed on your device.
  4. When you tap the “rate the app” prompt, which talks to the Play Store.

The requests in points 1 and 2 go directly to the sites you chose; we do not see or log them, and they are not used to identify you. Note that the servers of those sites inevitably see your device’s IP address, exactly as with any request you make from a browser. The app sends them no cookies, no advertising identifiers and no referrer information. It does send a standard browser-style User-Agent header, so that recipe sites serve their normal pages rather than an empty placeholder.

Requests in points 3 and 4 both go to Google, as described in this policy.

Permissions

The app declares a single permission of its own: INTERNET, which is needed for the network requests described above. The ML Kit libraries additionally declare ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, which lets the app check whether a connection is available before it downloads a language model.

The app requests no permission to access your photos, files, contacts, location, microphone or camera, and it cannot read anything on your device unless you choose it explicitly.

Photos are added in two ways. The photo picker lets you choose existing images from your gallery, and the app receives only the images you pick. “Take Photo” hands the job to your device’s own camera app, so the camera is controlled by that app and not by KokeKompis; KokeKompis itself declares no camera permission and never sees the camera.

Third-party software

Here is the complete list of the libraries KokeKompis is built on, and what each of them does:

  • Room — a local SQLite database. Your recipes are never sent to a server.
  • Jsoup and org.json — HTML and JSON parsing, entirely on your device.
  • OkHttp — the HTTP client used to fetch the pages and images you ask for.
  • Coil — the image loader that displays your recipe photos. Normally the app has already downloaded the photo and saved it together with the recipe; if that download failed, or if an imported recipe refers to a photo that is hosted on the web, Coil fetches it over the network and keeps a copy in the app’s cache folder.
  • ML Kit text recognition — reads text from photos. The model is bundled inside the app, so this works offline and nothing is sent anywhere.
  • ML Kit translation and language identification — translate recipes and detect the language of a recipe. The translation happens on your device; the language models are downloaded from Google the first time you use the feature and are then cached on your device.
  • Google Play in-app review — the “rate the app” prompt. It talks to the Play Store, which decides whether and when to show it.
  • AndroidX and Material 3 — user interface components only.

There is no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK and no telemetry library in the app. If a network request fails, the error message is written to your device’s system log; the log contains the error only, not your recipes, your photos or your settings.

If Google Play services is present on your device, it may keep its own technical diagnostics about the device for its own purposes, as it does for every app. KokeKompis neither reads nor collects that information.

This website

This policy is published as a static page on GitHub Pages. GitHub is the hosting provider and processes ordinary technical data — your IP address, browser and request time — in order to serve the page, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

The page itself sets no cookies, runs no analytics or advertising scripts, and loads no third-party resources. Your theme choice is stored only in your own browser’s local storage, and never leaves your device.

Google Play

The app is distributed through Google Play. Google is an independent controller of the data in your Google account and of the data generated by purchases and downloads through the store; that processing is governed by Google’s privacy policy. KokeKompis itself never sees your Google account, your purchase history, or who installed the app.

Legal basis (GDPR)

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland: the app processes your recipes and photos on your own device, and that processing is covered by our legitimate interest under Article 6(1)(f) GDPR in providing the app’s functionality on your device. We do not process special categories of personal data, and we do not use automated decision-making or profiling.

Your rights

Under the GDPR, the UK GDPR and similar laws, you have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict and export your personal data, to object to processing based on legitimate interests, and to withdraw consent where you have given it. You also have the right to complain to the supervisory authority in your country.

Because KokeKompis stores everything on your device and we hold no copy of it, you exercise almost all of these rights directly in the app: edit or delete a recipe, delete a photo, or uninstall the app. Anything that concerns us personally — for example a message you have sent us through the issue tracker — can be sent to us the same way, and we will action it.

International transfers

This page is served by GitHub from infrastructure located in the United States. To the extent that this constitutes a transfer of personal data outside the EEA, it takes place under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses. Your recipe data itself is never transferred anywhere.

Security

The app stores your data in your device’s private app storage, which the Android operating system isolates from other apps. The app is delivered and updated through Google Play, which verifies the app’s signature before installing it. The app connects to a web page using the address exactly as you give it, so a page you open over http:// is not encrypted — most recipe sites also offer https://, and we recommend you use those.

No method of transmission or storage is entirely risk-free. But the app sends us nothing, so there is nothing for us to hold on to: your recipes and photos never leave your device.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has contacted us, contact us and we will delete what they sent.

California (CCPA/CPRA)

The app collects no personal information about you automatically, and it sells or shares no personal information: there is no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no data brokerage of any kind. The data described in this policy is stored on your device and is not personal information that we collect, use or disclose within the meaning of the CCPA.

If you choose to contact us, you give us that information directly. Information you volunteer to a business is treated differently from information it collects or infers, and the CCPA’s exemptions for voluntarily provided information apply.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this URL with a new effective date. If a change is material — for example if the app ever starts collecting data it does not collect today — we will announce it in the app and on this page at least 30 days before it takes effect.
